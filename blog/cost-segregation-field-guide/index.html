Cost Segregation for Rental Property: Deduction and Usable Loss

By AE Tax Advisors | Updated October 1, 2026

Follow a cost-segregation analysis from property basis through asset classification, loss limitations and the eventual sale.

What a cost-segregation analysis does

Cost segregation evaluates components of depreciable property so assets are classified under the appropriate recovery rules. It can accelerate deductions for qualifying shorter-lived assets. Land remains nondepreciable. A defensible analysis needs purchase and improvement records, support for allocations, and correct asset classifications.

Start with basis and service dates

The closing date alone does not establish when a rental property is ready and available for its intended use. Confirm the placed-in-service facts, land allocation, acquisition costs and improvements. Review existing depreciation schedules before calculating a new deduction. The applicable bonus-depreciation rules depend on the property and relevant acquisition and service dates.

A deduction is not the same as current savings

For illustration, assume an analysis shifts $150,000 of depreciable basis into assets eligible for faster recovery. That is a classification figure, not a $150,000 tax refund. The current deduction depends on the recovery rules. The owner's use of a resulting loss requires a separate return-level analysis.

If a $150,000 deduction were fully usable against income taxed at an assumed 35 percent rate, the simple arithmetic would be $52,500. That assumption omits state tax, other limitations and future consequences. A suspended loss can produce a very different current-year result.

Review the loss limitations separately

Passive-activity and at-risk rules can restrict rental deductions. Where applicable, pass-through basis rules and excess-business-loss limits also need review. Short stays do not by themselves establish that every loss offsets wages. The activity's classification and the owner's participation must be supported by the actual facts.

Consider the holding period and exit

Accelerated depreciation changes timing and adjusted basis. A later sale can produce depreciation-related tax consequences. Evaluate the projected holding period, financing and sale alongside first-year cash benefits. The largest initial deduction is not necessarily the best lifetime outcome.

Records to prepare

  • Closing statement, land support and ownership documents.
  • Improvement invoices and a complete fixed-asset schedule.
  • Evidence of readiness for rental use.
  • Rental and personal-use records, participation evidence and income forecasts.
  • Prior depreciation detail and expected disposition plans.
