Rental Passive Activity Loss Rules: Allowance and Carryforwards

Separate rental loss allowances, MAGI, former passive activities and dispositions with checked fictional cases and clear carryforward boundaries.

July 15, 2026 · Updated November 22, 2026 · AE Tax Advisors

A rental tax loss, a loss currently allowed on a return and tax saved are different amounts. Keep current-year deductions, each limitation and prior-year carryforwards separate before estimating a result.

Begin with the right activity and limitation

Publication 925 explains that rental activities are generally passive, with exceptions. Active participation for the special allowance is different from material participation. Neither status alone establishes that every loss can offset wages. Basis and at-risk restrictions may apply before the passive-loss calculation; other limitations can apply afterward.

The special rental real estate allowance

The Form 8582 instructions describe the allowance for eligible active participants. The usual maximum for single or joint filers is $25,000, reduced by 50% of modified adjusted gross income (MAGI) above $100,000, reaching zero at $150,000. Eligibility includes an ownership-interest test and genuine management participation; ownership alone is insufficient.

Married-filing-separately rules differ. Filers who lived apart all year generally have a $12,500 maximum with $50,000–$75,000 phaseout thresholds; living together during the year generally prevents this special allowance. Use the applicable instructions rather than applying the single/joint formula to every filing status.

Original fictional allowance bridge

Assume a single filer with an eligible $28,000 rental real estate loss after preceding limitations, active participation, the required ownership interest, no passive income, no prior carryforwards or credits, and no other complications. The MAGI values are supplied as already computed under the applicable definition. These are not wages used as a substitute for MAGI.

In case B, the $25,000 excess MAGI reduces the maximum by $12,500: $25,000 − 0.5 × $25,000 = $12,500. The $28,000 loss then splits into $12,500 used and $15,500 suspended. None of these amounts is a tax credit or a calculated cash saving.

MAGI is not just wages minus deductions

Form 8582 has a specific MAGI computation. Some deductions, including deductible traditional IRA contributions, are not taken into account in that computation. A contribution does not necessarily restore the allowance merely because it reduces adjusted gross income elsewhere on the return. Have the preparer reconcile the actual MAGI calculation rather than using a salary or marginal rate alone.

Becoming nonpassive does not release every old loss

Section 469(f) and Publication 925 distinguish a former passive activity from its old suspended losses. Prior unallowed losses can offset current net income from that activity; the remaining prior loss continues under the passive rules. REPS qualification, a grouping election or an operating change is not a blanket release against wages.

For an original fictional example, assume one former passive activity has a $40,000 prior unallowed loss and $6,000 current net income before that offset. Assume preceding limits are satisfied and no other passive income, special allowance or disposition applies. The prior loss offsets $6,000, leaving $0 current net income from that activity after the offset and $34,000 of old loss still subject to passive rules. A new status did not convert all $40,000 into an unrestricted current deduction.

Compare this with the separate REPS qualification and participation guide. Keep the annual tests, the current activity’s treatment and the carryforward schedule as separate records.

A disposition needs its own review

Under section 469(g), full release generally requires disposing of the entire interest in a passive or former passive activity, recognizing all realized gain or loss, and an unrelated-party transfer. Selling one property in a larger grouped activity may not be a disposition of the entire activity. Gifts, deaths, partial dispositions and installment sales have special rules.

A tax-deferred exchange is not automatically that fully taxable disposition. Reconcile recognized gain and any losses under the applicable rules; do not simply mark the entire carryforward “released.” The exchange guide separately explains gain and replacement basis.

Build a loss rollforward before choosing a strategy

  • Beginning carryforward, identified by activity and type of limitation.
  • Current income or loss before carryforwards, with basis and at-risk support.
  • Participation, ownership and grouping facts for the year.
  • Applicable filing status and the actual MAGI reconciliation.
  • Loss used, remaining carryforward and support for any disposition treatment.

A short customer-use exception does not itself remove every loss limitation. Likewise, the bonus-depreciation amount is not proof the resulting loss is usable now.

Sources reviewed November 22, 2026: Publication 925 and Form 8582 instructions (2025 editions) and section 469 linked above. Use the applicable return-year rules and instructions. Original fictional calculations; no client result, tax savings or eligibility certification.
