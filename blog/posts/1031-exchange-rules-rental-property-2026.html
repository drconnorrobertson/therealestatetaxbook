1031 Exchange Rules for Rental Property in 2026

Review rental-property exchange deadlines, boot, recapture and replacement basis with two clearly fictional, reconciled gain examples.

July 1, 2026 · Updated November 22, 2026 · AE Tax Advisors

A qualifying section 1031 exchange can defer gain; it does not guarantee that every dollar of tax or depreciation recapture disappears. Start with the assets transferred, the transaction structure, the deadlines and a reconciled basis schedule.

What property is being exchanged?

IRS real estate tax tips explain that section 1031 applies to qualifying business or investment real property. Property held primarily for sale is excluded. U.S. real property and foreign real property are not like kind to each other. A personal residence or mixed-use property requires a separate analysis.

Identify each asset rather than assuming everything sold with a rental is qualifying real property. The Form 8824 instructions explain the section 1031 real-property definitions. Depreciation classification and exchange classification answer different questions. A short recovery period alone does not settle whether a component is real property for exchange purposes.

Record both deadlines from the transfer date

The general deferred-exchange rules require identification within 45 days and receipt by the earlier of 180 days or the return due date, including extensions. Both periods begin from the relinquished-property transfer; they are not added together. Confirm identification form, delivery and permitted-property limits with the exchange adviser. Do not assume a routine delay grants more time. Applicable IRS disaster relief may change a deadline for an eligible taxpayer; verify the specific relief rather than claiming there are no exceptions.

Arrange the exchange before closing

A qualified intermediary is a common safe harbor, not the only possible exchange structure. Publication 544 explains written agreements, disqualified persons and restrictions on actual or constructive receipt. Review the arrangement before transfer. Taking sale proceeds and later buying another property does not by itself create a deferred exchange.

Boot, recognized gain and basis are different numbers

Cash or non-like-kind property received can trigger gain recognition. Debt relief, debt assumed, cash paid and exchange expenses require their own net calculation. Do not apply a rule that replacement debt must always equal old debt: added cash may replace financing. Have the adviser reconcile the closing statements rather than rely on gross sale and purchase prices alone.

Original fictional example: a basis bridge, not a tax-saving claim

Assume qualifying real property, a valid exchange and no liabilities, personal property, related parties or additional recapture recognition. The invented property has $380,000 original basis and $78,000 depreciation, giving $302,000 adjusted basis. A $680,000 transfer less $20,000 of assumed qualifying exchange selling costs produces $660,000 net amount realized. Realized gain is $660,000 − $302,000 = $358,000. The expense treatment is an assumption for this exercise, not a classification of an actual closing charge.

Review recapture separately, especially after cost segregation

Section 1245 ordinary-income recapture, section 1250 ordinary recapture and unrecaptured section 1250 gain are not interchangeable. An exchange can require current recapture recognition; Form 8824 has a separate recapture calculation. Personal property does not become eligible for section 1031 merely because it accompanies the building. A limited incidental-property safe harbor does not grant tax deferral to that property.

Bring the asset-level depreciation schedule and sale allocation to the adviser. Review Form 4797 instructions and bonus-depreciation eligibility and timing alongside the exchange calculation. The fictional bridge above explicitly excludes additional recapture recognition and therefore cannot be used to promise a result for a cost-segregated property.

Build the transaction review file

  • Tax owner, entity documents and any proposed ownership changes.
  • Asset-level adjusted basis, depreciation and defensible sale allocations.
  • Exchange agreement, identification notice and evidence of timely delivery.
  • Both closing statements, debts, cash movements and itemized expenses.
  • Replacement basis calculation and records of gain recognized and deferred.

Have the adviser check taxpayer continuity, related-party rules and reporting. A last-minute ownership transfer or partnership exit is not automatically eligible. For a separate document trail, use the rental-sale depreciation and exit reading guide.
