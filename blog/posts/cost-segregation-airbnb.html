Cost Segregation for Airbnb Owners: Review the Study and Basis

Review an Airbnb cost-segregation report, reconcile purchase and furnishing costs, and separate asset support from bonus eligibility and usable losses.

June 29, 2026 · Updated November 22, 2026 · AE Tax Advisors

A cost-segregation study should explain which assets you own, how their costs were established and why their depreciation treatment is appropriate. A large projected deduction is not a substitute for that evidence. Start with the purchase and improvement records, then reconcile the report before considering bonus depreciation or tax savings.

What does a study establish?

Cost segregation separates costs into supported asset classifications. It does not turn every furnishing, fixture or outdoor feature into an automatically eligible deduction. IRS Publication 946 describes recovery periods, depreciation methods and conventions. The item’s function, the relevant asset or activity class, its relationship to the building and the applicable facts matter. Do not decide between five, seven or fifteen years from a marketing label alone.

The IRS Cost Segregation Audit Technique Guide, Publication 5653, explains examination considerations, including classification support and reconciling allocated costs to actual costs. Its cover states that it is not an official statement of law or the IRS’s position. The existence of audit guidance is not approval of an individual study.

Original fictional example: catch a duplicated invoice

Assume an invented $500,000 furnished-property purchase. A draft allocation assigns $100,000 to land, $260,000 to the building and $140,000 to other proposed asset groups. The supplied purchase inventory explicitly says that a $20,000 furnishings invoice is already included in the $140,000 group. The invoice is supporting documentation, not an additional purchase for this exercise.

Adding every displayed document amount would produce $520,000, overstating this purchase allocation by $20,000. Excluding the $100,000 land allocation leaves $400,000 of non-land allocation in the reconciled case, rather than the naive $420,000. Neither number is a validated depreciation deduction or a finding of tax basis for an actual property.

The tie-out does not prove that the $140,000 belongs in short recovery periods. It only shows where the same supplied cost was counted twice. A real furnishing invoice might instead be a separate purchase, a replacement, a personal-use item or an incomplete record. Establish its relationship to the purchase before deciding how to treat it.

Download the fictional reconciliation (CSV). It preserves the shown amount and counted amount separately. Do not total the “amount shown” column as though every row were an additional cost.

Ask for asset-level support

Give each report line a traceable source record. Ask which costs came from actual invoices, which were estimated and how shared or indirect costs were allocated. A total that ties can still contain unsupported classifications. A well-described asset can still have an unsupported cost.

Download a blank study review record (CSV). The proposed class, rationale, eligibility and review-state cells begin blank. They do not assign a recovery period to an item merely because it is furniture, lighting, a deck or a pool.

  • Match the legal owner, property, purchase date and study scope.
  • Tie the report’s cost totals to the closing statement, improvement ledger and asset records.
  • Separate supporting copies from additional costs, credits and replacements.
  • Request a classification rationale and identify unresolved asset facts.
  • Reconcile the proposed schedule to prior depreciation and any personal use.

Bonus depreciation is a separate eligibility review

IRS Topic 704 describes the 100% allowance for qualifying property acquired and placed in service after January 19, 2025. A study completed now does not reset an older asset’s original dates. The rate is not automatically 100% for every item on a study.

First-year building depreciation also depends on the applicable method and convention. Do not divide the building basis by its recovery period and automatically call that a full first-year deduction. Use the separate bonus-depreciation eligibility and timing guide for a checked mid-month example.

Review an older property’s correction procedure

A look-back study is not an automatic instruction to deduct all prior depreciation in the current year. Review the original service year, prior treatment, elections and the applicable correction procedure. Form 3115 instructions describe accounting-method change procedures, but eligibility, filing requirements and current procedural guidance need their own review. Some situations require a different correction route.

A deduction is not automatically usable savings

Publication 925 explains passive-activity and at-risk limits. The amount calculated on a depreciation schedule may not become a current deduction against wages. Review the allowance and carryforward guide rather than multiplying a projected deduction by a marginal rate and labeling it cash saved.

Also keep the asset-level history for sale or exchange review. A 1031 exchange does not guarantee every recapture item is deferred. Entity choice, participation and state treatment do not follow from a study’s classification table.

Compare actual proposals before commissioning work

Ask each provider to specify the property and tax years covered, records needed, methodology, deliverables, preparer experience, treatment of missing information, correction support and itemized fees. Identify what the return preparer will do separately. This guide supplies no typical allocation percentage, study fee or minimum property value; those require evidence relevant to the actual scope.

Sources reviewed November 22, 2026: Publication 946 and Publication 925 (2025 editions), IRS Topic 704, Publication 5653 (February 2025 examination guide) and Form 3115 instructions (December 2022 revision) linked above. Confirm current rules and procedures for the return being prepared. Original fictional reconciliation; no actual study, client result, asset eligibility or tax saving is certified.
