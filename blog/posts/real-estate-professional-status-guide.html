Real Estate Professional Status: Hours, Participation and Records

Review the REPS hour tests, spousal rules, material participation and prior losses with fictional boundary cases and a blank participation log.

June 29, 2026 · Updated November 22, 2026 · AE Tax Advisors

Real estate professional status (REPS) does not automatically make every rental loss deductible. Review annual qualification, participation in the relevant rental activity and the separate limitations on using a loss. Old suspended losses need their own schedule.

Two annual tests, each with a strict boundary

IRS Publication 925 requires more than 750 hours of services in real property trades or businesses in which you materially participate, and more than half of your personal services in all trades or businesses to be in those qualifying real property businesses. Exactly 750 hours is not enough; exactly half is not enough. A job title or property count does not prove either test.

Spouse, employee and activity rules answer different questions

Under Treasury Regulation 1.469-9, one spouse must separately satisfy the qualification requirements on a joint return. Two spouses’ 400-hour totals cannot simply become one person’s 800-hour qualification total. Employee services have a specific five-percent-owner rule; merely holding some ownership is insufficient.

Qualification does not replace material participation in the relevant rental activity. Each rental interest is generally separate unless an applicable election treats the interests as one rental real estate activity. Review the election’s scope, filing requirements, continuing effect and disposition consequences; it is not an automatic or universally beneficial annual choice.

Regulation 1.469-5T sets out material-participation tests. One uses more than 500 hours. Another uses more than 100 hours and participation not less than any other individual’s. Exactly 100 hours does not meet that second test. Spousal participation can count for material participation even though spouses’ service totals cannot simply be combined for the annual qualification tests.

Review the work, not just a category label

A calendar entry labeled “real estate” leaves important questions unanswered. Identify who worked, the activity, the actual task, the duration and the supporting record. Separate personal, investor and potentially countable operating work. Do not automatically count all education, deal research, tax planning or travel because the topic concerns property. The participation rules include restrictions on investor-type work.

Download a blank participation review log (CSV). Its review-state and rationale fields are blank so a task is not pre-approved merely by being entered. Record other business services separately for the annual comparison. Reconcile overlaps instead of counting simultaneous work twice.

The regulations allow participation to be established by reasonable means; a contemporaneous daily log is not the only permitted proof. Calendars, service descriptions and narrative summaries can matter. Regular, specific records make review easier, but a log does not make ineligible work count or guarantee acceptance.

REPS does not erase prior-year passive losses

Section 469(f) has separate former-passive-activity rules. Changing current status or making an election does not automatically release every old suspended loss against wages. Use the passive-loss guide’s former-activity example to keep the prior balance and current income separate.

For current losses, check basis, at-risk and other applicable limits as well as passive status. Corporate rules also differ: closely held C corporations and personal service corporations are within section 469’s scope. This individual qualification guide is not a reason to move rental property into a corporation.

Prepare the review file

  • Year-specific services for the individual being tested, including other business work.
  • Task-level support, disputed entries and the reason each task may count.
  • Relevant rental interests, participation by other individuals and spousal work identified separately.
  • Existing grouping statements and ownership or activity changes.
  • Current loss limits and prior-year carryforwards by activity.

For guest-stay exceptions, use the separate short-term rental participation guide. REPS, rental-activity exceptions and loss usability are separate reviews.
