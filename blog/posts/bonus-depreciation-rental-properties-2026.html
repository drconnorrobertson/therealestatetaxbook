Bonus Depreciation for Rental Property: Eligibility, Timing and Sale Review

Review 2026 rental bonus-depreciation eligibility, acquisition dates, placed-in-service timing, loss limits and sale records, with a fictional allocation.

June 29, 2026 · Updated November 22, 2026 · AE Tax Advisors

A rental purchase price is not automatically a bonus-depreciation deduction. First separate land, building and other assets; then establish acquisition timing, readiness for use and eligibility. A deduction is also different from a currently usable loss or a cash tax saving.

Which 2026 purchases can qualify?

IRS Topic 704 states the 100% special depreciation allowance applies to qualified property acquired and placed in service after January 19, 2025. The rule does not turn every earlier acquisition into 100%-eligible property. Notice 2026-11 addresses acquisition rules, including contracts and self-constructed property. Keep the signed contract and its conditions; the closing date alone may not resolve the acquisition test.

Under Form 4562 instructions, qualifying categories include certain MACRS property with a recovery period of 20 years or less. Certain used property can qualify, subject to acquisition and prior-use restrictions. Related-party or carryover-basis transactions need separate review. Land is not depreciable, and the ordinary 27.5-year residential rental or 39-year nonresidential building is not itself short-life bonus property.

Qualified improvement property has its own nonresidential-building and improvement requirements; an interior renovation is not automatically QIP. A seven-day average guest stay is a passive-activity question, not a rule automatically making a building 39-year property. Ask the preparer to document the building classification separately. See Publication 946 for depreciation classifications and the separate STR participation guide.

Readiness controls the placed-in-service record

Publication 527 explains that rental property is placed in service when ready and available for rent. Payment, closing, a study delivery date and an actual first booking can be different events. Retain dated records supporting readiness and the intended use.

Fictional allocation: reconcile the purchase before estimating a deduction

This original teaching example assumes a $600,000 purchase, $120,000 allocated to land, $144,000 of properly substantiated bonus-eligible assets and $336,000 of residential rental building basis. The allocation is invented, not a typical study percentage, property valuation or client result. Assume a 2026 acquisition, 100% income-producing use, eligibility for the 100% allowance, no election out, GDS treatment and no other basis adjustments. A real asset review could change these assumptions.

Residential rental building$336,00027.5-year straight-line, mid-month convention
Purchase allocation total$600,000$120,000 + $144,000 + $336,000

For a calendar-year taxpayer, compare two hypothetical placed-in-service months for this same allocation. January provides 11.5 months of first-year building depreciation: $336,000 ÷ 27.5 × 11.5/12 = $11,709.09. December provides 0.5 month: $336,000 ÷ 27.5 × 0.5/12 = $509.09. Adding the assumed $144,000 bonus amount gives $155,709.09 and $144,509.09, respectively. These are unrounded-formula teaching calculations displayed to cents, not prepared return amounts.

The $11,200 difference comes from the building’s first-year convention. Dividing its basis by 27.5 and calling that a full first-year deduction would miss the placed-in-service month. Publication 946 and Publication 527 explain the relevant depreciation methods and conventions. Neither scenario calculates federal or state tax savings.

What a cost-segregation study does not decide by itself

A classification study does not establish your usable loss, marginal tax rate or state result. Publication 925 describes at-risk and passive-activity limits. Other applicable limits and personal use also need review. A calculated depreciation amount does not automatically offset wages.

A later study of an older acquisition does not reset its acquisition or original service date. Have the preparer evaluate the original-year bonus rules, prior returns and the proper correction or accounting-method procedure. Do not assume a current study gives every historical component today’s percentage.

Plan for disposition as well as acquisition. Section 1245 ordinary-income recapture, section 1250 recapture and unrecaptured section 1250 gain are distinct concepts; a blanket 25% rate for every 15-year component is unreliable. The asset’s classification and depreciation history matter. Review Publication 544 and the exchange, boot and basis guide before assuming a later exchange defers every item.

Bring an evidence file to the review

  • Purchase agreement, amendments and closing statement.
  • Land/building allocation support and itemized asset costs.
  • Readiness records, invoices and business or personal-use history.
  • Prior depreciation schedules, elections and any study or method-change records.
  • Loss carryforwards, state adjustments and planned disposition questions.

Sources reviewed November 22, 2026. IRS publications and instructions have their own tax-year editions; confirm the rules and forms applicable to the return being prepared. This article is general education and does not determine an asset’s eligibility.
