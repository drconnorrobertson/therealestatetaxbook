Switching CPAs With Rental Properties: A Tax Record Handoff Checklist

Changing tax firms can improve planning, but a rental portfolio needs more than a PDF of last year's return. Before the new preparer starts work, agree on a transfer and reconciliation process for property basis, depreciation, loss carryovers and outstanding filings. This guide is for owners who have selected a new adviser and need to make the transition work.

Build a property and entity inventory

Create one list of every property, its legal owner, ownership percentage, use and return on which it appears. Include properties sold during the transition year and entities with no current rental income. Mark any building shared with your operating business, mixed-use property or investment reported through a partnership K-1.

The goal is to prevent scope gaps. A personal-return engagement may not cover partnership returns, state entity filings or bookkeeping cleanup. Have the new firm confirm the taxpayers and tax years included. Identify who owns each unfinished task before assuming the former preparer has stopped or the new preparer has taken over.

Request the detailed asset schedules

Obtain property-level depreciation schedules showing asset description, basis, acquisition and placed-in-service dates, recovery method and accumulated depreciation. Include land allocations, improvements, disposals and any cost segregation report. Preserve the source records supporting the schedules, including closing statements and invoices.

IRS Publication 527 describes rental depreciation and the information needed to calculate it. A single depreciation total from Schedule E does not give a successor preparer the full history. Ask the receiving firm to reconcile the detailed schedule to the latest filed return and identify differences before importing balances into its software.

Track carryovers by taxpayer and activity

Request the supporting schedules for suspended passive losses and any other relevant limitations, not just the final carryover total. Include prior-year computations and the activity descriptions used to track them. If a partnership interest is involved, ask what owner-level basis and at-risk records are available in addition to the K-1.

IRS Publication 925 explains passive-activity and at-risk rules. Different limitations should not be merged into one unlabeled loss balance. Have the new adviser state which carryovers have been reconciled, which remain provisional and what additional records are needed. A software import alone does not verify that a balance is correct.

Preserve elections, positions and unresolved issues

Ask for copies of elections and statements relevant to the rental activities, together with the returns on which they were filed. Identify grouping decisions, accounting-method work and any previous correction analysis. Provide notices, correspondence and a list of amendments or other filings still under consideration.

The incoming adviser should decide how prior positions affect the current return based on the actual documents. Changing firms does not automatically cancel an election or authorize a different treatment. If the new firm identifies a possible error, ask for a separate correction recommendation rather than allowing an unexplained opening-balance adjustment.

Make the transition deadline calendar explicit

  • Returns: list each taxpayer, jurisdiction, filing year and extension status.
  • Payments: identify estimated payments already made and who calculates the next amount.
  • Notices: record response dates and the professional responsible.
  • Sales: flag closings that require a tax projection before filing season.
  • Records: set a secure delivery process and a missing-document follow-up date.
  • Completion: confirm acceptance of the records and outstanding exceptions in writing.

Keep proof of filings and payments in your own records. Avoid giving two firms conflicting instructions to file the same return. An extension of time to file generally does not mean an extension of time to pay; ask the responsible preparer to address both obligations for the relevant return.

Use a short acceptance report

For a fictional owner with several rentals and a partnership investment, the handoff may be complete for directly owned properties but incomplete for the partnership's outside-basis history. The acceptance report should name that gap and its consequence. It is better to resolve a specific missing schedule than to hear that all documents were received when only the latest return was transferred.

Share detailed records through the agreed secure process. Ask whether reconciliation and historical cleanup are included in the proposed engagement or scoped separately.
